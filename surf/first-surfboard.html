Buyer's guide

Choosing a first surfboard

What actually matters when you are new: how stable the board is, how easily it catches a wave, and whether you will still want it next summer.

Soft-top or hardSizeA first setupRent first

The short version

  • Take a lesson or rent before you buy. It is the quickest way to learn what size suits you.
  • For most beginners, a long, wide, thick soft-top is the easiest board to learn on.
  • Bigger is friendlier. Most people who struggle early are on a board that is too small.
  • The board is only part of a first setup. Plan for the small things and for how you will carry and store it.

Soft-top or hard board

A soft-top (often called a foam board or foamie) has a foam deck and a slick plastic bottom. A hard board is the classic surfboard: a foam core wrapped in fiberglass and resin, or a molded epoxy shell. Both surf. They suit different stages.

Soft-top

Forgiving while you learn

  • A soft deck and soft rails are kinder when the board bumps into you, which it will.
  • Lots of foam means lots of float, so paddling and catching small waves is easier.
  • Shrugs off the knocks of learning and of being carried around.
  • Less responsive once you start turning, so some surfers move on from it.
Hard board

More feel, more care

  • Glides and turns with more precision once you have the basics.
  • Dings and cracks if dropped or knocked, and dings need repair before the board goes back in the water.
  • Harder surfaces mean harder bumps while you are still falling a lot.
  • A long hard board (a longboard) is still a fine board to learn on if you take care of it.

If you are unsure, start soft. It is the kind of board most lessons use, and for good reason.

Size, and how it relates to you

Three measurements decide how a board behaves: length, width and thickness. Together they set the board's volume, which is how much it floats. More volume means more stability, easier paddling and earlier entry into a wave. Less volume means a board that turns more easily but asks more of the surfer.

RiderWhat usually works
Adult beginnerA long, wide board with plenty of thickness. Many adults start on something in the 8 to 9 foot range. Go longer and thicker if you are taller or heavier.
Smaller or lighter adult, or a teenagerThe same shape with a little less length. The board should still feel big: easy to lie on, hard to tip.
ChildA shorter soft-top that the child can carry and control, used with an adult close by. Follow the maker's size and weight guidance for the exact board.
Returning surferBe honest about how long it has been. A longer board gets you back on waves faster than the one you rode years ago.

Height matters less than weight and fitness. A board's maker publishes its dimensions and usually its volume and a suggested rider weight. Use those figures, not a guess, and ask a shop to check your choice.

Why small local waves favor a bigger board

Summer surf at Wrightsville Beach and Carolina Beach is usually small. Small waves have less push, so a board with more float catches them more easily. A big board is not a compromise here. It is the right tool.

What a first setup involves

We do not quote prices, because they vary by maker, size and season and they change. In general terms:

  • The board is the main cost. Soft-tops generally cost less than hard boards of the same length. Longer boards generally cost more than shorter ones of the same build.
  • Used boards cost less than new. Check a used hard board carefully for cracks, soft spots and old repairs, or bring someone who knows what to look for.
  • Fins. Many beginner boards come with fins. Check before you assume.
  • A leash. It keeps the board near you after a fall. It is not a flotation or rescue device, and it does not replace being able to swim.
  • Wax or a deck grip for a hard board, so your feet stay put. Many soft-tops are ridden without wax.
  • A wetsuit if you plan to surf outside the warm months.
  • Getting it to the beach. A long board needs a secure way to travel on or in your car, and somewhere out of the sun to live at home.

Add those up before you commit. The small items and the transport are where first-time buyers get surprised.

Why a lesson or a rental comes first

A lesson includes a board and starts on the sand before you paddle out. In one session you find out how a big soft-top feels under you, whether you like it, and what an instructor thinks you should ride next. Renting a couple of different sizes after that costs far less than buying the wrong board.

When you are ready to buy, a local surf shop can fit you in person and knows the waves you will be riding. ShoreList does not sell surfboards.
